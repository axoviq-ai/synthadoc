# Mental Models

Powerful frameworks for thinking and decision-making. Each model page records:

- **Model name** — the standard name; originating domain (economics, psychology, physics, biology, etc.)
- **Core insight** — the model in one sentence; what it reveals that ordinary thinking misses
- **How to apply** — a two-to-four step process for using this model in practice; what inputs it takes and what output it gives
- **Example** — a concrete, memorable example of the model in action
- **Common misapplications** — how people misuse this model; what they mistake it for; when it does not apply
- **Origin** — the thinker or discipline associated with this model (link to [[thinkers]] and [[books]])
- **Related models** — other mental models that complement or contrast with this one (link to [[concepts]])

Organize entries by the cognitive function each model serves:
- **Decision-making** — models for choosing well under uncertainty
- **Problem-solving** — models for finding root causes and solutions
- **Systems thinking** — models for understanding feedback and emergence
- **Probabilistic thinking** — models for reasoning about likelihood and risk
- **Communication** — models for understanding how people process information

Cross-link to [[concepts]] for atomic ideas the model connects to, [[thinkers]] for its originator, and [[books]] where you first encountered it.
