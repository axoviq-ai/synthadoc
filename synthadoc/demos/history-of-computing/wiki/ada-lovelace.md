# Ada Lovelace

Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace (1815–1852), commonly known as Ada Lovelace, was a British mathematician and writer. She is widely regarded as the first computer programmer for her work on Charles Babbage's proposed Analytical Engine. ^[computing-pioneers-timeline.xlsx:17-17]

## The Analytical Engine and the First Algorithm

In 1843, Lovelace published what is considered the first algorithm intended for machine execution. Her notes on Luigi Menabrea's memoir about babbage's Analytical Engine included a detailed method for computing Bernoulli numbers using the machine. This work introduced concepts such as loops and conditionals — programmatic constructs that would not become standard until the 20th century. ^[computing-pioneers-timeline.xlsx:3-3]

## Vision of General-Purpose Computing

Beyond her specific algorithm, Lovelace is celebrated for her visionary insight that computing machines could go beyond mere calculation. She envisioned the Analytical Engine as a general-purpose device capable of manipulating symbols, composing music, and producing graphics — anticipating the [[von-neumann-architecture]] idea of stored-program, general-purpose computers by more than a century. ^[computing-pioneers-timeline.xlsx:17-17]

## Legacy

The Ada programming language, developed in the late 1970s for the U.S. Department of Defense, was named in her honour. Ada Lovelace Day, observed in mid-October, celebrates the achievements of women in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM). Her work is a foundational reference point in the history of [[programming-languages-overview]] and the broader history of computing. ^[computing-pioneers-timeline.xlsx:17-17]
